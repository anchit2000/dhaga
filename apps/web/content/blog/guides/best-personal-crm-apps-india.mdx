Every "best personal CRM" list I've read was written for a US reader with a US credit card and no second thought about a $12/month subscription. In India the maths lands differently. A $12/month personal CRM is roughly ₹12,000 a year for an app that remembers your contacts — and half the good ones don't have a real free tier, are iOS-first, or keep your entire relationship history on a server you'll never see.

So this is the version I wish existed: a fair look at the personal CRM apps worth your time in 2026, with an Indian lens on price, free tiers, and where your data actually lives. I build one of these tools (Dhaga), so I'll be upfront about where it wins and where it plainly doesn't. The goal is that you can decide in five minutes.

## How to choose a personal CRM (five criteria)

Before the list, here's the framework. A personal CRM is relationship *memory* — private notes about people you know — not a sales pipeline. Judge every option on these five things, in this order:

1. **Privacy and data ownership.** This is your private intelligence on third parties: who introduced whom, who said they'd invest, who's worth a warm intro. Can you export it all? Can you host it yourself? For Indian users thinking about data residency under the DPDP Act, self-hosting is the only way to guarantee your relationship data stays on infrastructure you control.
2. **AI and natural-language recall.** The whole point is asking "who do I know in fintech in Bengaluru?" and getting an answer, instead of scrolling. Some tools do this well, some bolt it on, some don't do it at all.
3. **Capture methods.** How does a person *get into* the system? Business-card scan, voice note, LinkedIn import, email sync, browser extension, or pure manual entry. The lower the friction, the more you'll actually use it.
4. **Price — read in rupees.** A US SaaS sticker of $10–20/month is a real recurring cost here. Weigh the free tier and the annual plan honestly, and check whether the tool takes payment in a way that works for you.
5. **Self-host / open-source.** Optional for most, decisive for some. Open-source means the project outlives its startup, and self-hosting means zero per-seat cost and full control.

## The best personal CRM apps in India for 2026

I've covered six tools that come up again and again. None of them is bad. They're built for different people.

### Dex

Dex is probably the most well-known dedicated personal CRM, and for good reason: it's clean, it syncs your LinkedIn connections and email, and it's genuinely good at "keep in touch" reminders and a running timeline of every interaction. It has started adding AI-assisted notes and drafting too. The catch for Indian users is price and payment: Dex is subscription-only (in the region of ~$12/month, roughly ₹1,000/month) with a trial rather than a permanent free tier, and it's a closed cloud app — your history lives on their servers. If a polished, sync-everything experience is worth the recurring USD to you, Dex is a strong pick.

### Mesh (formerly Clay)

Mesh (at me.sh — the personal CRM formerly called Clay, before its 2025 acquisition by Automattic) is the most automated option here. It quietly ingests your contacts from email, calendar, LinkedIn, and other accounts and auto-builds a rich, self-updating profile for each person, then nudges you to reconnect and flags relationships going cold. Its AI assistant, Nexus, searches your network and drafts follow-ups in plain English. That hands-off, zero-effort capture is its superpower and, for the privacy-minded, its main tradeoff — a lot happens automatically, in a closed cloud you don't control, with no self-host option. It has a free tier up to 1,000 contacts and a Pro plan around $10/month, but no India-specific pricing or localization. Great if you value automatic capture over data minimalism.

### Folk

Folk sits at the boundary between a *personal* CRM and a lightweight *team* CRM. Its browser extension pulls contacts straight off LinkedIn, it has pipelines and shared groups, and it added AI for enrichment and message drafting. That team-shaped design is also why it's the priciest here — plans typically start around $20/user/month (~₹1,700). If you're a solo operator it's more tool than you need; if you and a co-founder want a shared, light CRM, it's worth a look.

### Notion (as a CRM)

Notion isn't a CRM — it's a flexible database you can *shape* into one, and plenty of Indian founders and professionals do exactly that on the free personal plan. The upside is obvious: free, infinitely customizable, and you already have it. The downside is everything a real personal CRM does for you, you now do yourself — no card scan, no voice capture, no automatic follow-up reminders, and no relationship recall unless you pay for the Notion AI add-on and it answers generically over your pages. Perfect if you love building your own system; frustrating if you want the system to do the remembering.

### Monica

Monica is the open-source elder of personal CRMs, and it's excellent. It's AGPL, self-hostable, and free if you run it yourself — which makes it the honest answer for anyone who wants full data ownership without a subscription. It's built around logging interactions, reminders, relationships, even gift and journal tracking. What it deliberately *isn't* is AI-native or graph-native: there's no "ask it in plain English," no automatic business-card or voice capture, and no warm-intro pathfinding. If you want a private, self-hosted relationship journal and you're happy to type, Monica is superb. If you're specifically looking for a **Monica CRM alternative in India** that keeps the privacy and adds AI plus capture, read on.

### Dhaga

Dhaga is the tool I build, so treat this as a disclosed pitch rather than a neutral verdict. It's aimed at the same person Monica is — someone who wants to own their relationship data — but it's AI-native and graph-native from the ground up. You capture people however is fastest (business-card scan, voice note on the web app, a quick paste of an email signature, a browser extension, even a Telegram bot), and Dhaga turns those notes into a private knowledge graph you query in plain English: *"who did I meet at the Bengaluru fintech dinner who mentioned they angel invest?"* It finds warm-intro paths through your own network, and it's open-source (AGPL) with a one-command `docker compose up` self-host, so your data can stay on infrastructure you control — inside India if you want. The honest caveats: cloud AI is the paid part (the free tier covers manual capture, notes, and organization, with Pro around $8/month), and the native Android and iOS apps are still in progress — today the mobile surface is an installable PWA, so **personal CRM on Android in India** works but isn't yet a native Play Store app. If privacy plus AI plus self-host is your exact bundle, it's built for you.

## Which one should you actually pick?

Map it to how you work:

| If you are… | Start with |
|---|---|
| A founder living on warm intros who wants AI + privacy | Dhaga |
| Someone who wants a proven, polished cloud CRM and will pay for it | Dex or Mesh |
| A solo operator who wants free and fully in your control | Monica (self-host) |
| A tinkerer who wants to build your own system for free | Notion |
| Two co-founders wanting a shared light CRM | Folk |

> The best personal CRM for professionals in India isn't the one with the most features — it's the one whose free tier and privacy model you can live with for years, because a relationship memory is only valuable if you keep feeding it.

A note for **founders** specifically: your network *is* your fundraising and hiring pipeline, and it's the asset most likely to be scattered across WhatsApp, LinkedIn, and a phone contacts list that remembers nothing. Whatever you pick, pick something you'll open weekly — the tool that gets used beats the tool with the best feature matrix every time.

## The privacy angle, and where Dhaga fits

If you strip away branding, the real fork in this list is cloud-convenience versus data-ownership. Dex, Mesh, and Folk are excellent cloud apps that do a lot for you in exchange for holding your data. Monica and Dhaga are the open-source, self-hostable side — you keep the data, at the cost of a little setup (or by using a hosted tier and trusting the code is open for anyone to audit).

Dhaga's specific bet is that you shouldn't have to choose between "private" and "smart." It keeps every AI-derived fact tied to the note it came from (delete the note, the derived facts go with it), makes cloud AI opt-in and metered rather than always-on, and lets you export everything or run the whole thing yourself. That's the differentiation — not that it maps your connections (plenty of tools do), but that it does the private, natural-language, own-your-data version of it.
