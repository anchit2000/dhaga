You did the responsible thing and scanned all of them, so now they're digitized business cards sitting neatly in an app. Names, titles, phone numbers, all correct.

And then nothing happens. You never message forty people. You message four. The other thirty-six slowly turn into strangers with job titles, and the next time you actually need one of them — the fintech founder, the investor who said "keep me posted," the operator who offered to intro you to their VP — you can't remember which card was which.

This is the real problem with business cards, and no amount of OCR accuracy fixes it. So this guide does two things: it compares the actual **business card scanner** options in India honestly, on the dimensions that matter for a scanner (accuracy, offline, free, CRM sync), and then it names the dimension the whole category quietly ignores.

## What actually makes a card scanner good

There are five things people search for when they want to **scan a business card to contacts**, and four of them are about the scan itself:

- **OCR accuracy.** Does it read the card correctly — including the awkward ones, the two-line company names, the handle that's actually an email? This is the core job.
- **Works offline.** Can you scan on airplane mode or on flaky conference Wi-Fi, without the image going to someone's cloud first? A **business card scanner offline** matters more in India than most vendors admit, given venue connectivity.
- **Free.** Is there a real **business card scanner app free** in India tier, or does the useful part sit behind a subscription?
- **CRM sync.** Does the contact flow into where you actually work — Salesforce, HubSpot, a spreadsheet, your phone's address book? **Card scanner CRM sync** is what separates a toy from a tool.

The fifth thing nobody puts on the box: **does the scan lead anywhere?** Six months later, does the app help you remember the person and nudge you to reach back out — or did it just file them?

Hold that fifth one. First, the honest comparison.

A few honest notes on each, because a matrix flattens nuance:

### ABBYY Business Card Reader

The OCR benchmark. Best-in-class recognition across many languages, on-device processing (so it genuinely works offline), a proven one-time-purchase model, and clean export into Salesforce and other CRMs. If your single requirement is "read the card perfectly and get it into my CRM," this is hard to beat. What it doesn't do is remember anything *about* the person — it's a frozen, excellent scanning utility.

### CamCard

The long-standing **OCR business card app** most people have heard of. Strong recognition, batch scanning, exchange-your-card features, and mature **card scanner CRM sync** into the big sales tools. The free tier is limited and the product is cloud-centric, so your cards live on their servers — fine for many people, a consideration if privacy matters to you.

### BizConnect

The India-forward option, built around scanning and pushing leads into a CRM. It has a usable free tier and leans into the "digitize business cards India" use case with local-market polish and integrations. Solid choice if you want a scanner that already speaks CRM out of the box.

### Google Lens

The default free answer. It's on almost every Android phone and every iPhone via the Google app, it reads text well, and it costs nothing — the honest **best card scan app Android** starting point if you just need to pull a number off a card occasionally. But Lens is a general text tool, not a card tool: it doesn't structure the fields into a proper contact, it mostly needs connectivity, and there's no CRM sync or memory layer at all.

> Every one of these nails capture. Not one of them is designed to still be useful to you in November about someone you met in March.

## OCR accuracy: who actually wins

Let me be blunt, because the whole category over-indexes on this and I'd rather you spend on the right thing.

If you are scanning hundreds of cards and need near-perfect field extraction with zero manual correction, a dedicated scanner — ABBYY or CamCard class — is the right tool, and the **best card scanner Android 2026** shortlist should start there. They've spent years on exactly this problem: multi-line layouts, mixed scripts, logos that look like text, phone formats. That's real engineering and it deserves the credit.

Dhaga does not claim to beat them at OCR. Dhaga's card scan is "good" — it reliably reads a clean card and pulls out name, title, company, email, and phone, with an edit-before-save step so you confirm the fields before anything is saved. It's more than enough for the pace at which a founder actually collects cards. But if raw scan throughput is your job, use a specialist. This guide isn't going to pretend a personal CRM out-scans a scanner.

The point is that OCR accuracy is a solved problem you can buy cheaply. The unsolved problem is downstream.

## The column nobody scores: what happens after the scan

Here's the reframe. A business card scanner's job, as the category defines it, ends the instant the contact is saved. Card in, contact out, done. But that's the moment your actual problem *begins* — because a saved contact with no context is just a nicer-looking version of the card you were going to lose anyway.

## How to choose

A quick, honest decision guide:

- **You just need to pull a number off a card now and then.** Google Lens. It's free and it's already on your phone.
- **You scan cards in bulk and need flawless extraction into Salesforce/HubSpot.** ABBYY, CamCard, or BizConnect. Buy the specialist.
- **You keep losing people after you scan them — the follow-up never happens, the context evaporates.** That's not an OCR problem, and a better scanner won't fix it. You need something that treats the scan as the *start* of a relationship, not the end. That's the personal-CRM lane.
