If you've been shopping for a personal CRM, you've hit Dex (getdex.com). It's the most well-known dedicated one for a reason: roughly 30,000+ users, a clean product, and a pitch that lands — "one place for your relationships," built for people, not a sales pipeline. If you're weighing it against an open-source challenger, this is the honest Dex-vs-Dhaga breakdown, written by someone who builds the challenger. I'll be upfront about where Dex plainly wins, because it wins a lot.

The short version of the tension: Dex is a managed cloud product that captures your network *for* you. Dhaga is a private, open-source graph you feed and own. Those are two genuinely different bets, and the right one depends entirely on what you value.

## The core difference: automatic capture vs. private ownership

Everything else is downstream of this one split.

**Dex's bet is convenience.** It plugs into the accounts where your relationships already live — Gmail/Outlook (two-way email sync), Google Calendar, LinkedIn — and quietly builds your CRM without you typing much at all. Its most impressive trick: automatic **job-change detection**, so when a contact moves companies, Dex notices and nudges you to reach out. That is a real, mature, hard-to-build feature, and it's the beating heart of the product.

**Dhaga's bet is ownership.** It's an AI-native personal CRM built privacy-first: you capture people as you go, those notes become a private knowledge graph you query in plain English, and the whole thing is open-source (AGPL) and self-hostable. The tradeoff is the honest headline of this whole comparison: **Dhaga has no live email, calendar, or LinkedIn sync today.** You get contacts in through manual add, a LinkedIn Connections CSV export, a browser extension, a paste of an email signature, or a Telegram bot — not an ambient background feed.

So if the single most important thing to you is that your CRM fills itself, stop reading and get Dex. That's not false modesty; it's the truth, and it's what Dex is best in the world at.

## Where Dex wins (and it's not close on these)

Being fair here matters, because Dex is the incumbent and it earned the position. Where it's clearly ahead of Dhaga right now:

- **Automatic capture from email, LinkedIn, and calendar.** The whole value prop, and it's mature. Two-way Gmail/Outlook sync, calendar sync, LinkedIn auto-sync (up to 9,000 connections on the top plan), iMessage/social sync, two-way phone-contact sync. Dhaga has none of these as live sync.
- **Automatic job-change detection + reach-out notifications.** Dhaga has no live equivalent shipping today.
- **Mature, polished native mobile + desktop.** Dex has real iOS and Android apps (with business-card scan and AI voice notes on mobile) and a desktop app. Dhaga's mobile surface today is an installable PWA — the native apps are still in progress.
- **AI pre-meeting briefs from your calendar.** Dex Copilot / Ask Dex does natural-language recall and CRUD ("who did I meet at the Google event in product?"), plus calendar-aware meeting prep and daily digests.
- **A shipped MCP server + agent skill, today.** You can point Claude or ChatGPT at Dex and have it operate the CRM. Dhaga now ships an MCP server too — `/api/mcp`, ten tools, and the read half costs no AI credits — so what's left of the gap here is Dex's packaged agent skill and its longer mileage, not the connection itself. (Dhaga's write tools are additive only: no delete, no merge, no bulk actions.)
- **Power-user plumbing.** Mail merge, Zapier, and a public API on the Professional plan.
- **Brand, reliability, and a big user base.** ~30,000+ users and years of polish. A newer open-source project can't hand you that on day one.

Dex is also privacy-*respecting* in the ways that matter to most people: it's supported by subscriptions rather than ads, says it never sells your data, and gives you full export and delete. The one thing it can't offer — because it would break its own model — is self-hosting.

## Where Dhaga wins

Now the other side, honestly. These are real advantages, and most of them are exactly the things Dex *structurally can't* match without abandoning its cloud model.

- **Open-source and self-hostable.** Dhaga is AGPL with a one-command `docker compose up`. Your entire relationship graph can live on infrastructure you control — inside India if data residency matters to you. Dex is closed-source central cloud; there's no self-host, and there never can be without changing what Dex is.
- **A free tier that's actually usable.** This is the sharpest contrast. Dex's free plan exists but is deliberately thin: unlimited contacts, but *basic reminders only* — no integrations, no AI, and no mobile app. Dhaga's free tier is a real working tool: manual capture, notes, follow-up reminders, warm-path finding, and search across your own graph, at zero cloud-AI cost. (Dhaga's AI features — natural-language recall, card and voice capture, drafting — are the paid tier, around $8/month. Cloud AI is opt-in and metered, never always-on.)
- **Fact-level provenance and cascade delete.** Every AI-derived fact in Dhaga keeps a receipt — the note it came from. Delete that note and its derived facts, edges, and embeddings go with it. "Forget this person" cascades completely. That's a privacy invariant built into the schema, not a settings toggle.
- **Warm-intro pathfinding at zero AI cost.** Dhaga walks your own graph — shared companies, events, and relationships — to find the shortest warm path to someone you want to reach. It's local graph traversal, so it costs nothing and reveals nothing to a model.
- **Cheap capture rails with no integration or subscription.** A Telegram bot, a paste box, a LinkedIn CSV import, a browser extension. None of it requires wiring up an OAuth integration or paying for a tier.
- **India + founder/operator/VC positioning.** Built for people who live on warm intros and read pricing in rupees, not just US knowledge workers.

## Pricing, read honestly

Dex's real prices: **Free** ($0, basic reminders only). **Premium** ~$12/month billed annually ($144/year) or $20/month monthly — this is the tier that unlocks LinkedIn, Gmail, calendar, AI, and mobile. **Professional** ~$20/month annually adds LinkedIn up to 9,000 connections, Outlook, mail merge, Zapier, and the API. There's a 7-day trial and up to a 50% student discount.

For an Indian reader, the Premium tier is roughly ₹12,000 a year — and note that the *useful* Dex (integrations + AI + mobile) starts at Premium, not free. Dhaga's free tier is a genuinely usable manual CRM at ₹0, with AI on a ~$8/month Pro plan if and when you want it. If you're comparing total cost of ownership across the field, our roundup of the [best personal CRM apps in India](/blog/guides/best-personal-crm-apps-india) puts both in context with Monica, Mesh, and Notion.

## Which should you choose?

This one maps cleanly. No hand-waving:

| If you… | Choose |
|---|---|
| Want zero-effort auto-capture from email/LinkedIn/calendar | **Dex** |
| Want polished native mobile + desktop today | **Dex** |
| Will happily pay ~$12/mo for a managed product that "just works" | **Dex** |
| Require privacy, data ownership, or self-hosting | **Dhaga** |
| Prefer open-source you can audit and run yourself | **Dhaga** |
| Want a genuinely useful *free* manual tier (AI only if you opt in) | **Dhaga** |
| Are a founder/operator/VC in India living on warm intros | **Dhaga** |

> The real question isn't which tool has more features. It's whether you'd rather your CRM fill itself in someone else's cloud, or stay entirely yours on infrastructure you control.

## The takeaway

Dex is the safer, more finished product, and if automatic capture is the feature you'd trade everything else for, it's the right call — a Dex alternative only makes sense if you're optimizing for something Dex doesn't give you. But if you searched for a getdex alternative because you want ownership, an open-source codebase, self-hosting, and a free tier that isn't crippled, that's a different tool with a different bet.

That tool is [Dhaga](/) — free to start, open-source to inspect, and yours to run. Just go in clear-eyed: you're trading Dex's automatic sync for control, not getting both. Today, that's the honest deal.
