<Callout title="The short version">
OpenVC and Dhaga get compared because both live near a fundraise — but they're different categories of tool. OpenVC is a fundraising platform: a curated database of thousands of investors you can filter, pitch, and track through a raise. Dhaga is a private personal CRM: a relationship graph of everyone *you already know*, kept as searchable memory you own. One helps you find and reach investors; the other helps you remember every relationship for life. If you're raising, the honest answer is usually "use both."
</Callout>

If you searched *dhaga vs openvc* — or landed here looking for an *OpenVC alternative* — you're probably mid-raise, staring at your runway math, trying to figure out which tool actually solves your problem. Here's the most useful thing I can tell you up front: these two barely overlap. Comparing them feature-for-feature is a little like comparing a directory of investors to your own memory of the people you've met. Both are about relationships. They do opposite jobs.

This guide lays out what each one is genuinely good at, where each falls short, and why — for a founder raising a round in India or anywhere else — the smart move is usually to run both rather than pick one.

## They're not the same category

**OpenVC** (openvc.app) is a fundraising platform. Its own tagline is "raise capital for your startup from ~16,600 investors," and that's exactly the job. It gives founders a curated, verified investor database you can search by stage, sector, geography, and check size, then reach out and track through your raise. It's founder-facing and, by their own numbers, used by around 40,000 founders. OpenVC exists to help you **find investors** for the round you're running *right now*.

**Dhaga** is a personal CRM — a private knowledge graph of the people you already know. You capture contacts and context from anywhere (a business-card scan, a voice note after a coffee, a web quick-add, a browser extension, your own LinkedIn connections export), and it stitches those notes into a graph you can query and keep for years. It has no external investor directory and never will — it only knows people you've actually met. Dhaga exists to help you **remember** relationships across every raise, every hire, every role.

So the real question was never "which one wins." It's "which job are you doing?" — and often the answer is both. OpenVC sources people you *don't* know. Dhaga remembers the people you *do*.

## What OpenVC does well

A comparison that trashes the other tool is worthless, so let me be straight about OpenVC's strengths — several of them are things a personal CRM structurally cannot do:

- **A curated, verified investor database.** Roughly 16,000+ investor profiles, filterable by geography, check size, stage, industry, and even reply rate. This is cold-investor *discovery* — sourcing people you don't know yet — and it's the thing Dhaga fundamentally can't offer.
- **In-platform outreach.** Submit your deck to investors who accept inbound, and run cold outreach without leaving the tool.
- **A native fundraising CRM.** A proper raise pipeline to track which investor sits at which stage of your process.
- **Deck hosting + engagement analytics.** Host your pitch deck and see opens, time-per-slide, which slide people stopped on, and where they viewed it from — real signal during a live raise.
- **An AI pitch-deck reviewer.** Slide-by-slide feedback on your deck.
- **Intro Finder (Premium).** Connects your Gmail and LinkedIn to surface mutual-connection **warm intros to investors** specifically.
- **Strong India coverage.** Dedicated India / Bengaluru / Delhi investor pages — which matters a lot if you're raising here.
- **A genuinely generous free plan.** Around 90% of the features — database search, the fundraising CRM, deck hosting, and one AI deck review — are free. Premium ($99/mo or $299/yr) adds 5 outreaches a day, 10 filters, Intro Finder, unlimited AI reviews, plus masterclass and partner perks.

One honest note on OpenVC's AI: it's deck review and investor matching. It is *not* natural-language recall over your own notes and relationships. Different AI, different job — which is exactly the seam between these two tools.

## Where Dhaga is the better tool

If your job is remembering relationships for the long run — not sourcing a cold investor list — Dhaga does things OpenVC isn't built for:

- **A private graph across *all* your relationships, not just investors.** OpenVC is scoped to the raise. Dhaga holds customers, hires, mentors, ex-colleagues, and the friend who casually mentioned they "do a bit of angel stuff" — the whole network, in one place you can query. Most warm intros you'll ever get come from people who were never on any investor list.
- **Ongoing relationship memory instead of transactional history.** OpenVC is built around *this* raise. Dhaga is built to outlive it: capture what an investor actually said, log the objection as its own fact with a receipt back to the note it came from, set a keep-in-touch cadence so the relationship doesn't quietly decay after the round closes. The investors who pass this time are your warm list for the next one — but only if something remembers them.
- **Multi-surface capture.** Scan a badge at a demo day, dictate a voice note in the taxi home, quick-add from the browser, bulk-import your LinkedIn connections — every path lands in the same graph.
- **You own the data — you can self-host it.** This is the sharpest contrast. OpenVC's Intro Finder works by ingesting your Gmail and LinkedIn into its cloud. Dhaga is open-source (AGPL core) and self-hostable — a `docker compose up` and your relationship graph runs on your own infrastructure. A sensitive raise doesn't have to live on someone else's servers.

The flip side, stated plainly: Dhaga only knows people you already know. It has no cold-investor discovery, no deck hosting, no slide analytics, and no raise pipeline. If your problem is "I don't know enough investors," Dhaga is the wrong tool for *that* half of the problem.

## Use both: OpenVC finds the investor, Dhaga remembers the relationship

The cleanest way to think about it is a handoff. OpenVC is brilliant at the front of the funnel — discovering and reaching investors you don't yet know. Dhaga is built for everything downstream — turning those investors (and everyone else you meet) into durable, private memory you can reuse for years.

In practice: you use OpenVC to build a target list and get in front of partners you've never met. The moment a conversation happens, you capture it in Dhaga — who they are, what they said, the objection they raised, the follow-up you promised — and Dhaga starts tracing warm-intro paths from *your own* network to the funds on your list, because a two-hop warm intro still beats a cold submission. (We go deep on that in [warm introductions and mutual connections](/blog/guides/warm-introductions-mutual-connections).) OpenVC's day-one value is that it works with *zero* of your own data. Dhaga's value compounds precisely *because* it's your data, accumulating across every raise and role.

## So which should you pick?

A simple rule:

- **Choose OpenVC when the job is "raise *this* round."** You need to discover and reach cold investors, host and track a deck, and run a fundraise pipeline. That's its whole reason to exist, and the free plan makes it a low-risk yes.
- **Choose Dhaga when the job is "own my relationship intelligence for life."** You want a private, self-hostable **investor CRM** — and contact CRM — that remembers every conversation, surfaces warm paths, and answers questions in your own words, long after any single raise is over.
- **Use both, and it's not redundant.** OpenVC to find and reach investors; Dhaga as the durable private graph of everyone you meet along the way — those investors included — so next time you're raising, the wall of *"I don't know any VCs"* simply isn't there. For the discipline of running that relationship layer through a raise, see [investor relationship management for startups](/blog/guides/investor-relationship-management-startups).

## The takeaway

*Dhaga vs OpenVC* was always the wrong framing. OpenVC is a fundraising platform that finds and reaches investors you don't know; Dhaga is a personal CRM that remembers the ones you do — privately, queryably, and on infrastructure you can own. They meet at exactly one point: the warm intro. Use OpenVC to fill the top of your raise, and let [Dhaga](/) turn every person you meet along the way into a private graph you'll still have when the next round starts.
