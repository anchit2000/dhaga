title: Calendar & reminders

**`/app/calendar`** lays every open follow-up out on a full-screen calendar, so
you can see what's coming instead of scrolling a list. Dated follow-ups land on
the grid; date-less ones wait in a tray you drag onto it. It's an extra way to
work the same follow-ups you already set — nothing new to maintain.

![The follow-ups calendar](/docs/guide/calendar.png)

## Month and list views

Two views share a toolbar at the top — **month** for the grid and **list** for a
week-at-a-glance agenda — with **prev / next / today** to move around. The
calendar opens on the month grid on desktop and the list view on a phone,
whichever reads better on the screen you're on.

## Reschedule by dragging

Pick up any follow-up and drop it on another day to move its due date. The
change saves as soon as you let go — if anything goes wrong it snaps back and
tells you. No dialog, no form: just drag it where it belongs.

## The unscheduled tray

Follow-ups you logged without a due date don't disappear — they gather in an
**Unscheduled** tray above the calendar, each as a chip with the contact's name
and what you meant to do. Drag a chip onto a day to give it a date; it leaves the
tray and takes its place on the grid. Collapse the tray with its header when you
want it out of the way.

## Open a follow-up's details

Click any item — on the grid or in the tray — to open its details. From there
you can:

- **Open contact** — jump straight to the person's page.
- **Mark done** — close the follow-up out.
- **Dismiss** — clear it without marking it done.

![A follow-up's details dialog opened from the calendar](/docs/guide/calendar-event.png)

## Birthdays and anniversaries

Any **important date** you've saved on a contact — a birthday, a work
anniversary, the day you met — shows up here too, on the day it comes round each
year. They sit on the grid as soft amber all-day entries reading
*"Priya — Birthday"*, with a quieter second line for the age or count where the
saved date includes a year.

They behave differently from follow-ups, on purpose:

- **They aren't draggable and they can't be marked done.** A birthday isn't a
  task you complete — it's a fact about a person. Drag one and it snaps straight
  back.
- **You edit them on the person.** Click one and Dhaga opens the contact; the
  date lives under **More details → Important dates**, where a calendar picker
  (with a year dropdown, so a 1974 birth year is two clicks) sets it. Change it
  there and the calendar follows.
- **They're worked out on the fly**, never stored as separate reminders. Fix a
  date or forget a person and nothing is left behind to clean up.

Dates you imported from Google Contacts or a `.vcf` sometimes arrive as plain
text — *December 9*, or a day and month with no year. Dhaga keeps those exactly
as they came and still works out when they next fall; it never quietly rewrites a
date you didn't type.

How far ahead a date counts as "upcoming" is yours to set, under **Suggestions**
in [Settings](/docs/guide/settings) — 7 days by default.

## Your connected calendar, alongside

By default a connected calendar tells Dhaga only when you're **busy** — blocks
of time, no titles, no attendees — and Dhaga never writes to it. That stays true
unless you say otherwise.

If you **upgrade** a connection in [Settings](/docs/guide/settings), two things
change for that calendar and nothing else:

- **Its events show up here**, next to your follow-ups and styled differently so
  you can always tell which is which. They're read-only in Dhaga — to change one,
  change it in the calendar it came from. Only a window around today is fetched,
  so events far in the past or future won't appear.
- **Your follow-ups get written out** to a separate calendar Dhaga creates,
  called **Dhaga**. Never your main calendar — so you can hide it, or delete it
  outright, without touching anything of your own. Reschedule a follow-up and its
  event moves; mark it **done** or **dismiss** it and the event is removed.

Both are per-connection and per-account: upgrading one calendar leaves any other
connection exactly as it was. You can switch the write-out off on its own, from
Settings, and keep the connection.

<Callout title="Event details stay on your screen">
Titles, locations and guests from a connected calendar are only ever displayed.
Dhaga doesn't store them, doesn't log them, and doesn't send them to an AI model.
</Callout>

## Staying reminded

You don't have to keep the calendar open to stay on top of things:

- The **bell** in the top nav opens **Notifications** — one short list holding
  three things: follow-ups **due today** or **overdue**, birthdays and
  anniversaries coming up, and word that a background job has finished (a note
  you added has turned into facts, an enrichment has landed). Mark a follow-up
  **done** right there, dismiss a notification with its **×**, or jump to the
  full calendar or your follow-ups list. When it's empty, you're all caught up.
  The **count on the bell** is deliberately narrow — things to act on *now*: what's
  overdue or due today, anything unread, and a date only once it's actually
  arrived. A birthday next week is in the list without nagging you.
- **`/app/follow-ups`** gains an **Upcoming dates** block below your open
  follow-ups, showing who's next and how far off. It's simply absent when nothing
  is coming.
- Turn on the **daily reminder email** to get a morning nudge whenever follow-ups
  are waiting — it now reaches you while there's still time to act, covering
  anything due in the next few days rather than only what's already late.
- Turn on the separate **birthday and anniversary email** to hear about a saved
  date before it lands. You'll get at most two per occasion: one when it comes
  into view, one on the day.

Both emails are opt-in and both start off — flip them on under **Suggestions** in
[Settings](/docs/guide/settings), where you can also set your **time zone** so a
day starts and ends where you are.

<Callout title="The calendar is an extra view, not a replacement">
Your **Home** briefing and the **`/app/follow-ups`** list still show the same
follow-ups — the calendar just adds a spatial, drag-to-reschedule way to see
them. Use whichever fits the moment.
</Callout>
