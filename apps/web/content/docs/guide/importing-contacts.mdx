---
title: Importing contacts
description: Bring an existing address book into Dhaga — from a .vcf or CSV file, a one-click Google or Outlook connection, or the contacts already on your phone.
---

Already have an address book somewhere? Import it instead of re-typing. Dhaga
takes it three ways: a **file** you export (works everywhere, no setup), a
**one-click connect** to Google or Outlook, and **on-device import** in the
mobile app. Existing people are matched and skipped, and every imported field
keeps a **receipt** so you can trace where it came from — a permanent note on
the contact, not something you can edit away.

Open **`/app/import`** (it lands you on **Settings → Import**), or scroll to the
**Import contacts** section on `/app/settings`.

![Dhaga's Import contacts screen: a drop area for a .vcf or CSV file, a Choose file button, and collapsible per-source export instructions](/docs/guide/import-contacts.png)

## From a file (.vcf or CSV)

Drop a `.vcf` (vCard) or `.csv` onto the import area, or click **Choose file**.
The file is **parsed entirely in your browser** — nothing is uploaded until you
pick who to keep. You then get a **review list** to select rows, and only the
selected contacts are saved.

The screen has collapsible **"How to export from…"** steps for each source. In
short:

- **iPhone / iCloud** — on a computer, sign in to **iCloud.com → Contacts**,
  press **⌘A / Ctrl+A** to select everyone, then the gear (bottom-left) →
  **Export vCard**. (On the phone you can share a single contact as `.vcf`, but
  iCloud.com exports everyone at once.)
- **Android** — **Contacts app → Settings → Export → Export to .vcf file**, then
  move it to your computer.
- **Google** — **contacts.google.com → Export** and choose **vCard** or
  **Google CSV** (both work here).
- **LinkedIn** — click **Get contacts from LinkedIn** on the import screen to open
  LinkedIn's **"Get a copy of your data"** export page in a new tab (or go there
  yourself via **My Network → Connections → Manage synced contacts**). LinkedIn
  emails you the archive — usually about a day later — then you upload the
  `Connections.csv` here.
- **Outlook** — export contacts as CSV from Outlook and drop the file in.

<Callout title="Dhaga reminds you to finish a LinkedIn import">
LinkedIn doesn't hand you the file on the spot — it emails the archive about a
day later, which is easy to forget. After you click **Get contacts from
LinkedIn**, Dhaga sends a short "did your export arrive? upload it here" email on
days 1, 3, 6, and 7, then stops — and stops sooner the moment you import your
LinkedIn contacts. The reminders only go out on instances with email configured;
a self-host without email set up simply skips them.
</Callout>

<Callout title="Big address books">
Very large files (100k+ contacts, tens of MB) will warn you and can be slow —
split the `.vcf` into a few smaller files if it drags.
</Callout>

## One-click: Connect Google or Outlook

When the instance is configured for it, a **Connect an account** row appears
above the file drop with **Connect Google Contacts** and/or **Connect
Outlook / Hotmail** buttons. Clicking one sends you to that provider's own
consent screen — you're granting **your own account**, read-only, and you can
disconnect any time. On return, your contacts come straight into the same
review-and-select list as a file import.

- **Google** covers Google Contacts.
- **Outlook** covers Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live, and Microsoft 365.

<Callout title="These buttons only show when configured">
The connectors need a one-time operator setup (OAuth credentials). If you don't
see them, the instance hasn't wired them up — the file import above covers every
source in the meantime. Operators: see [Self-hosting](/docs/self-hosting) and the
repo's `docs/CONTACT_IMPORT_SETUP.md` for the Google Cloud / Azure steps.
</Callout>

## No Apple / iCloud connector

There's no "Connect iCloud" button — Apple provides no contacts API. For
Apple/iCloud contacts, use the **`.vcf` export** from iCloud.com described above;
it brings in everyone at once.

## On your phone (mobile app)

The mobile app can import the contacts **already on the device**. Open the
**Import contacts** screen, grant the **Contacts** permission when asked (iOS and
Android each show a native prompt; if you decline, the screen offers an **Open
Settings** shortcut), then pick who to bring in. It uses the same API key you set
up on the mobile Setup screen — no extra configuration.

<Callout title="Needs a native build">
On-device import ships as code but needs a native app build to appear (it can't
arrive via an over-the-air JS update or plain Expo Go), and is pending on-device
testing.
</Callout>

## What happens to the non-people you bring in

An address book is never only people — "Ola Support", "Vegetable Vendor", "AC
Repair" come in with everyone else. Dhaga looks over the imported rows you never
do anything with and marks the ones that read as a business or a service, so
they stop turning up in the Home briefing and the other places Dhaga volunteers
a name.

**Nothing is deleted or hidden.** Those rows stay in **People**, in search, in
duplicate detection, in your Wrapped card and in every export — the only change
is that Dhaga stops suggesting them. The People page shows an *"N hidden from
suggestions"* link that opens exactly those rows, each contact says whether the
call was Dhaga's guess or yours, and one tap overrules it for good. Anyone
you've starred, noted, or reached out to is never judged at all. See
[your daily briefing](/docs/guide) for the full behaviour.

Once people are in, add [notes and facts](/docs/guide/notes-and-facts),
[enrich them from the web](/docs/guide/enrich-from-web), or group them into
[events](/docs/guide/groups-and-events).
