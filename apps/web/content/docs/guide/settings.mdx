**`/app/settings`** is where you tune how Dhaga behaves and connect it to the
rest of your tools.

![The Dhaga settings page with its Account, Capture, Calendar, Suggestions, and Import tabs, showing the Account tab's Profile card (editable name and read-only sign-in email) above the Security section](/docs/guide/settings.png)

## What you can configure

Settings is grouped into tabs — pick a tab along the top and its sections sit
beneath it.

- **Account** — your **Profile** (edit your display name; your sign-in email is
  shown read-only and can't be changed here), **Appearance** (the app's colour
  theme and font — see below), **Billing** and plan, **Security**
  (two-factor authentication, 2FA), and **API keys** — personal access tokens for
  the API and the browser extension, and for scripted
  [exports](/docs/self-hosting/deploy#backups--migration). On a hosted instance
  the Billing section also shows a one-line **AI credits** summary — *"3 of 10 AI
  credits used"*. Free accounts have a monthly allowance like any other: **10
  credits a month**. The line only reads *"No monthly AI credits on this plan —
  upgrade to enable AI actions"* if whoever runs the instance has explicitly set
  your allowance to 0, which isn't the normal free-tier state. The full picture
  lives in its own tab, next door.
- **Credits** — your AI-credit ledger: how many are left this month and when they
  reset, a breakdown of where the rest went by kind of action, and a list of your
  most recent AI actions. Credits are charged per action, not per model call: a
  card scan, note, quick-add or draft costs 1; a question to Ask Dhaga or a
  pre-meeting brief costs 2; enriching a contact from the web costs 20. The
  nightly passes — watchlist scans, contact checks and goal matches — are free;
  they show in the breakdown marked *Free* rather than being hidden. Credits aren't the only gate: enrichment and pre-meeting briefs
  are Pro-and-up features whatever your balance says. Your allowance can be
  raised by whoever runs the instance — a
  promotional month, or credits granted to you after something went wrong on
  their side — and extra credits are only ever *added* on top: what you spent is
  a record and is never rewritten. Full walkthrough:
  [AI credits](/docs/guide/ai-credits). Operators: those controls are the admin
  AI credits screen, documented in
  [Self-hosting](/docs/self-hosting#instance-wide-ai-credit-controls-appadminai-credits-hostedee).
- **Capture** — **Card photos** (whether the original card image is kept after a
  scan) and **Voice teaching** — a vocabulary manager where you teach Dhaga
  names it keeps mishearing. The fastest way to teach a word is tap-to-fix right
  in the dictation transcript; anything you correct there shows up here.
- **Calendar** — connect Google or Outlook so Dhaga can work against your
  schedule. A new connection reads your **busy times only** — no titles, no
  guests — and writes nothing. Each connection can be **upgraded** on its own,
  from a second consent screen, to also show its events on
  [your calendar](/docs/guide/calendar) and write your follow-ups to a separate
  calendar called **Dhaga** (never your main one). Upgrading is per calendar, and
  the write-out has its own off switch.
- **Suggestions** — the daily reach-out suggestions and email digest that feed
  your Home briefing. The people-per-day number is a **ceiling, not a quota**:
  on a day your calendar is already full of meetings Dhaga shows fewer, and it
  never drops below one person — a busy day is the day you most need the
  reminder. Also here: a **morning follow-up reminder** email that nudges you
  when you have follow-ups or check-ins waiting, and an opt-in **Confirmations
  digest** email (off by default) that summarises the AI confirmations waiting
  for your review. The same tab holds **Birthdays & anniversaries**: how many
  days ahead a saved date counts as upcoming (0–90, default 7), and an opt-in
  reminder email that arrives that far in advance. **Background job alerts** sits
  alongside them: an opt-in email for when a note extraction or web enrichment
  **fails or is blocked** by your AI budget, at most one every 15 minutes. Runs
  that succeed are not emailed — every job, successful or not, is recorded in
  your notifications, and a run that worked asks nothing of you. Like every email
  preference in Dhaga these start off — the first-run walkthrough stops here so
  you choose, and nothing is switched on for you. It also holds your **Time
  zone** — see below.
- **Import** — bring an existing address book into Dhaga (see below).

<Callout title="Some sections depend on configuration">
Billing only appears when the instance has Stripe configured, and calendar/
email features need their providers wired up. On a plain self-host several of
these sections are intentionally absent — see [Self-hosting](/docs/self-hosting).
</Callout>

## Appearance

**Account → Appearance** — or the direct link **`/app/settings#appearance`** —
changes how the Dhaga app looks: a **colour theme** and a **font**, both yours
alone. It's also where the first-run walkthrough's settings leg opens — you can
click a theme while the tour is still on screen.

![The Appearance card in the Settings Account tab: a "Colour theme" grid of theme options with three colour swatches each, followed by a Font dropdown and Reset to default button](/docs/guide/settings-appearance.png)

### Pick a theme

Eight themes ship: **Dhaga** (the calm blue-white/midnight default), **Dhaga
Classic** (the original warm paper/charcoal and amber identity), **Monochrome**,
**High contrast**, **Rose**, **Ocean**, **Forest** and **Violet**. Pick one and
the app repaints immediately — there's no Save button on this card.

<Callout title="A theme changes the colours, not light vs dark">
**Light and dark stay where they always were** — on the toggle in the top bar.
Every theme here ships *both* a light and a dark palette, so whichever you pick,
the toggle keeps doing exactly what it did: a theme decides the hues, the toggle
decides which of its two palettes you see.
</Callout>

![The Dhaga Home briefing rendered under the Monochrome theme — the same metric tiles, sparklines, daily briefing and follow-ups as usual, with every amber accent replaced by neutral greys on a black-and-white ground](/docs/guide/app-theme-monochrome.png)

![The Dhaga Home briefing rendered under the Rose theme — the same layout again, this time on a deep pink-tinted ground with rose accents on the buttons, sparklines and highlighted rows](/docs/guide/app-theme-rose.png)

The list is a fixed set rather than a free colour picker on purpose. Every
theme's palette is contrast-tested in CI on every change — body and secondary text and
accent text against WCAG AA, and control borders against the 3:1 minimum — in
both of its modes. A hand-mixed colour can't be held to that, and unreadable
timestamps are a much worse outcome than a shorter list.

### Pick a font

The **Font** dropdown offers **Geist Pixel** (Dhaga's own display face, and the
default), **System** (your OS's own UI font — downloads nothing), and **Inter**,
**Roboto**, **Open Sans**, **Lato**, **Montserrat** and **Poppins**. The
dropdown and each option render in the face they name, so you can see the
choice before you make it. Nothing is downloaded until you actually open the
list — staying on Geist Pixel costs you no extra font download at all.

### It follows you, and it stops at the app

Your choice is saved **against your account on the server**, not in the browser
— so it's there on your laptop, your phone browser and any machine you sign in
on next, and clearing your browser data doesn't lose it. Because it's stored
server-side, the app arrives already wearing your theme: there's no flash of the
default palette while a page loads.

It applies to **`/app` only**. The public site, this documentation and the blog
keep Dhaga's own colours. That's deliberate rather than an omission — those
pages are pre-rendered and shared with everyone, and making them per-reader
would mean rendering them fresh on every visit and giving up their page speed.

**Reset to default** puts both back: the Dhaga theme and Geist Pixel. It's
greyed out when you're already on both.

## Connect your AI assistant

**Account → Connect your AI assistant** sits directly above the **API keys**
card. It shows two things, each copyable in one click: this instance's **MCP
endpoint**, and the **`npx skills add`** command that teaches an assistant how
to use Dhaga. A token from the card below it is what a client running on your
own machine — Claude Code, Cursor — signs in with; claude.ai and ChatGPT connect
as a connector instead and need no token at all. Which route is yours, and what
to paste where, is in [Connect your AI assistant](/docs/guide/mcp).

## Your time zone

Under **Suggestions**, **Time zone** is the setting that decides where your day
begins and ends. Search for a city or region, or take the **Use detected zone**
shortcut when Dhaga notices your browser disagrees with what's saved — it fills
the box for you and waits; nothing changes until you press Save.

It matters because reminders are about *days*. With it set, a birthday reaches
you on the day it is where **you** are, rather than wherever the server happens
to be, and a reminder you've already had can't arrive again.

<Callout title="It picks the day, not the hour">
Everything still goes out on one daily run, so the *time* an email lands is set
by the instance, not by your zone. Your time zone decides which **day** a
reminder belongs to — not what time of morning it arrives.
</Callout>

New accounts start on **UTC** until you choose one, so nothing shifts under
anyone who hasn't looked at this yet.

## Import your contacts

The **Import** tab brings an existing address book into Dhaga. Drop a **`.vcf`
(vCard) or CSV** export (Google, LinkedIn, Outlook, iCloud) and it's **parsed in
your browser** — it doesn't get uploaded somewhere to be processed. Where the
instance is configured for it, **one-click Google or Outlook** connectors pull
your contacts in directly, and the **mobile app** can import the contacts already
on your phone. The imported people land in your graph ready to enrich with notes.
See [Importing contacts](/docs/guide/importing-contacts) for the full walkthrough.
